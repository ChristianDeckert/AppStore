Test with CRTCam

  1. Use a monitor that runs at 100 Hz or more, and set its brightness to full. Dimmed backlights often flicker on their own, and CRTCam may lock onto that instead.
  2. Pick a rate with a green light, press Start, then full screen. Fill CRTCam's frame with the screen.
  3. Tap Detect in CRTCam. It should find the SCAN rate shown here, not your monitor's refresh.
  4. Pick a wrong preset in CRTCam to see bands, then the right one to see them go.

Safari on a 120 Hz Mac draws at 60 Hz unless you turn off "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" under Develop › Feature Flags. Chrome and Firefox draw at the full rate.

What a browser can't do

  • No real beam. A browser draws one picture per refresh of your monitor, so the page lights the band of lines the beam crossed during each frame. That's two steps per scan on a 120 Hz screen and four on 240 Hz.
  • Whole frames only. Other rates would flash at a slow beat, which no CRT does, so the page won't run them. A 60 Hz monitor can't show any CRT rate.
  • Your monitor flickers too. LCD panels update top to bottom during each frame, and many dim with PWM. CRTCam may detect those instead of the scan.
  • Uneven frames show as stray lines. Plug in, close other tabs, and turn off variable refresh (G-Sync, FreeSync, adaptive ProMotion) if the display rate wanders.
  • Dimmer while scanning. The panel can't flash brighter than its maximum, so the picture averages half brightness on 120 Hz and a quarter on 240 Hz.
