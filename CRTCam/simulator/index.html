Two modes

REAL SCAN

For rates that take a whole number of your monitor's frames, two or more: 60 and 59.94 Hz on a 120 Hz screen, 50 Hz on 100 Hz, 60 and 120 Hz on 240 Hz. The page redraws the beam, so the light really flickers at the scan rate. CRT Retro Cam's Detect finds that rate and the shutter sync clears the bands. This is the mode for testing.

ROLLING BARS

For every other rate, and for every rate on a 60 Hz screen. One or two soft dark bars roll slowly down the test picture, the way a phone camera shows a CRT. Use it to see the effect, try CRT Retro Cam's controls and take demo shots. The bars are drawn into the picture, so CRT Retro Cam can't sync them away, and Detect won't find a CRT rate. To test the sync itself, use real scan or a real CRT.

Test with CRT Retro Cam

  1. Use a monitor that runs at 100 Hz or more, and set its brightness to full. Dimmed backlights often flicker on their own, and CRT Retro Cam may lock onto that instead.
  2. Pick a rate with a green light, check that the mode reads REAL SCAN, press Start, then full screen. Fill CRT Retro Cam's frame with the screen.
  3. Tap Detect in CRT Retro Cam. It should find the SCAN rate shown here, not your monitor's refresh.
  4. Pick a wrong preset in CRT Retro Cam to see bands, then the right one to see them go.

On a 60 Hz screen every rate runs as rolling bars: fine for trying the app and taking demo shots, not for testing Detect or the sync.

Safari caps pages at 60 fps, even on 120 Hz ProMotion screens, unless you turn off its "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" feature flag. On a Mac it is under Develop › Feature Flags (turn on "Show features for web developers" in Safari's Advanced settings to get the Develop menu). On iPhone and iPad it is under Settings › Apps › Safari › Advanced › Feature Flags. Reload the page afterwards. Chrome on a 120 Hz Mac runs at 120.

What a browser can't do

  • No real beam. A browser draws one picture per refresh of your monitor, so the page lights the band of lines the beam crossed during each frame. That's two steps per scan on a 120 Hz screen and four on 240 Hz.
  • Whole frames only. A real scan at other rates would flash at a slow beat, which no CRT does, so the page draws rolling bars for them instead. A 60 Hz monitor can't scan any CRT rate.
  • Your monitor flickers too. LCD panels update top to bottom during each frame, and many dim with PWM. CRT Retro Cam may detect those instead of the scan.
  • Uneven frames show as stray lines. Plug in, close other tabs, and turn off variable refresh (G-Sync, FreeSync, adaptive ProMotion) if the display rate wanders.
  • Dimmer while scanning. The panel can't flash brighter than its maximum, so the picture averages half brightness on 120 Hz and a quarter on 240 Hz.
